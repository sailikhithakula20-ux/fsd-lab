# Preventive Healthcare and Chronic Disease Nutrition Guidelines
# Compiled from peer-reviewed clinical literature and WHO/ICMR guidelines

## Type 2 Diabetes Prevention and Nutritional Management

### Dietary Risk Factors
- Excessive consumption of refined carbohydrates (white rice, maida, refined sugar) is the primary dietary driver of insulin resistance in the Indian population
- High glycaemic index (GI) meals cause rapid postprandial blood glucose spikes, stressing pancreatic beta-cells
- Sugar-sweetened beverages increase Type 2 Diabetes risk by 26% per daily serving (Malik et al., 2010, Diabetes Care)
- Trans fats from vanaspati ghee, deep-fried snacks, and commercial bakery products impair insulin signalling

### Evidence-Based Protective Strategies
- Replace polished rice with hand-pounded rice, brown rice, or millets (ragi, jowar, bajra) to reduce glycaemic load by 20-40%
- The traditional Indian meal pattern of dal + rice/roti provides complementary amino acids and slow-digesting protein-starch matrices
- Fenugreek seeds (methi) soaked overnight: 15-25 g daily reduces fasting blood glucose by 15-20 mg/dL (Gupta et al., 2001, JAPI)
- Bitter gourd (karela) contains polypeptide-p, an insulin-mimetic compound
- Chromium-rich foods: whole grains, broccoli, green beans support glucose tolerance factor
- Cinnamon (dalchini): 1-3 g daily modestly reduces fasting blood glucose in some clinical trials
- Physical activity: 150 minutes per week of moderate aerobic exercise reduces diabetes incidence by 58% (Diabetes Prevention Program, 2002)
- Post-meal walking: a 15-minute walk within 30 minutes of finishing a meal reduces postprandial glucose peaks by 12-22%

### Glycaemic Index Reference (Indian Foods)
- Low GI (<55): rajma (19), chana dal (11), soya bean (15), apple (36), curd (36), milk (27), green vegetables (15), ragi dosa (55)
- Medium GI (55-69): basmati rice (58), chapati (62), oats porridge (55), banana (52)
- High GI (>70): white rice (73), white bread (75), potato (78), watermelon (76), puffed rice (82)

## Cardiovascular Disease Prevention

### Lipid Management Through Diet
- Limit total dietary fat to 25-30% of energy, saturated fat to less than 7%
- Replace saturated fats (ghee, butter, coconut oil) with monounsaturated fats (olive oil, groundnut oil, mustard oil) and polyunsaturated fats (sunflower oil, flaxseed oil)
- Omega-3 fatty acids from flaxseeds (alsi), walnuts (akhrot), chia seeds reduce triglycerides by 15-30%
- Soluble fibre from oats, psyllium husk (isabgol), fenugreek seeds, barley lowers LDL cholesterol by 5-10%
- Garlic (2-4 cloves daily) has modest cholesterol-lowering and antiplatelet effects
- Green tea polyphenols may reduce LDL oxidation

### Blood Pressure Management (DASH Diet Adaptation for India)
- DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) reduces systolic BP by 8-14 mmHg
- Sodium restriction: limit to less than 2000 mg per day (approximately 5 g salt or one level teaspoon)
- Average Indian salt intake: 9-12 g per day (2-3 times WHO recommendation)
- High-sodium Indian foods to moderate: pickles (achar), papads, chutneys, namkeen snacks, processed cheese, instant noodles
- Potassium-rich Indian foods for BP management: coconut water, banana, spinach (palak), sweet potato, tomatoes, buttermilk (chaas)
- Magnesium-rich foods: pumpkin seeds, almonds, cashews, dark chocolate, spinach
- DASH components: 8-10 servings of fruits and vegetables daily, 2-3 servings of low-fat dairy, whole grains, nuts, and legumes
- Regular aerobic exercise: 30-60 minutes most days of the week reduces systolic BP by 4-9 mmHg

### Cardiac Emergency Warning Signs (Education)
- Chest pressure or squeezing sensation lasting more than 5 minutes
- Pain radiating to left arm, jaw, back, or upper abdomen
- Shortness of breath with or without chest discomfort
- Cold sweats, nausea, or lightheadedness during exertion
- Action: call emergency services immediately (108 in India)

## Obesity and Weight Management

### BMI Classification (Asian Indian Criteria)
- Underweight: BMI below 18.5
- Normal weight: BMI 18.5 to 22.9
- Overweight: BMI 23.0 to 24.9
- Obese Class I: BMI 25.0 to 29.9
- Obese Class II: BMI 30.0 and above
- Note: Asian Indians have higher visceral adiposity at lower BMI compared to Western populations, hence lower cutoffs

### Sustainable Weight Loss Principles
- Caloric deficit of 500 kcal per day results in approximately 0.5 kg weight loss per week
- Protein preservation during caloric deficit: 1.2 to 1.6 g protein per kg body weight
- Extreme caloric restriction below 1200 kcal/day is medically unsafe and causes muscle wasting
- Indian meal timing: avoid late-night heavy meals; finish dinner 2-3 hours before bedtime
- Portion control: use smaller plates (thali), serve dal and vegetables first, rice or roti second
- Mindful eating: chew each bite 20-30 times; takes 20 minutes for satiety hormones to reach the brain

## Sleep, Circadian Health, and Metabolic Function

### Sleep Duration Recommendations
- Adults (18-64 years): 7 to 9 hours per night
- Elderly (65+ years): 7 to 8 hours per night
- Chronic sleep deprivation (less than 6 hours) increases ghrelin (hunger hormone) by 15% and decreases leptin (satiety hormone) by 15%, leading to increased caloric intake

### Sleep Quality Optimization
- Maintain consistent sleep and wake times, including weekends
- Avoid caffeine (tea, coffee, cola) after 2 PM
- Limit blue light exposure (smartphones, laptops) 60 minutes before bedtime
- Keep bedroom temperature at 18-22 degrees Celsius
- Evening warm milk with a pinch of turmeric (haldi doodh) contains tryptophan that supports melatonin synthesis
- Physical activity improves sleep quality but intense exercise should be completed at least 3 hours before bedtime

## Hydration and Kidney Health

### Daily Water Requirements
- Baseline: 35 ml per kg body weight per day
- Warm climate adjustment: add 500-1000 ml in summer months
- Physical activity adjustment: add 500-750 ml per 30 minutes of moderate exercise
- Signs of dehydration: dark yellow urine, dry mouth, headache, fatigue, dizziness

### Indian Hydration-Supportive Foods
- Coconut water: natural electrolyte balance, 250 ml contains approximately 600 mg potassium
- Buttermilk (chaas): hydrating, contains probiotics, and traditional digestive aid
- Watermelon and cucumber: 92-96% water content
- Lemon water (nimbu pani) with minimal sugar and a pinch of rock salt
- Avoid excessive tea and coffee as caffeine has mild diuretic effects
