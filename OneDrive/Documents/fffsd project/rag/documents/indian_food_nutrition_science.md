## South Indian Staple Foods and Nutritional Profiles

### Rice-Based Preparations
- Steamed White Rice (100g cooked): 130 kcal, 2.7g protein, 28g carbs, 0.3g fat, GI: 73
- Brown Rice (100g cooked): 123 kcal, 2.7g protein, 26g carbs, 0.9g fat, GI: 50, higher fibre (1.8g)
- Pongal (100g): 140 kcal, 4.5g protein, 22g carbs, 3.8g fat (moong dal + rice + ghee)
- Lemon Rice (100g): 165 kcal, 2.8g protein, 25g carbs, 5.2g fat
- Curd Rice (100g): 110 kcal, 3.5g protein, 18g carbs, 2.5g fat (probiotic benefits)

### Dosa and Idli Family
- Plain Idli (1 piece, ~40g): 39 kcal, 1.6g protein, 7.8g carbs, 0.2g fat, fermented (enhanced B-vitamin bioavailability)
- Masala Dosa (1 piece, ~120g): 206 kcal, 3.8g protein, 25g carbs, 9.5g fat
- Ragi Dosa (1 piece, ~80g): 120 kcal, 3.2g protein, 18g carbs, 3.5g fat (calcium-rich: ~180mg)
- Rava Idli (1 piece, ~50g): 78 kcal, 2.0g protein, 12g carbs, 2.5g fat
- Uttapam (1 piece, ~100g): 160 kcal, 4.0g protein, 22g carbs, 5.5g fat (vegetable-loaded version preferred)

### Millet Preparations (Siri Dhanya)
- Ragi Mudde (100g): 110 kcal, 2.0g protein, 22g carbs, 0.7g fat, calcium: 344mg/100g raw ragi
- Jowar Roti (1 piece, ~50g): 95 kcal, 2.8g protein, 18g carbs, 1.0g fat, high fibre
- Bajra Roti (1 piece, ~50g): 100 kcal, 2.5g protein, 17g carbs, 1.5g fat, iron-rich
- Foxtail Millet Upma (100g): 130 kcal, 3.0g protein, 23g carbs, 2.5g fat, low GI

### Sambar, Rasam, and Accompaniments
- Sambar (100ml): 65 kcal, 3.2g protein, 8.5g carbs, 1.5g fat, toor dal base with vegetables
- Rasam (100ml): 32 kcal, 0.8g protein, 5.0g carbs, 0.5g fat, digestive spices (pepper, cumin, tamarind)
- Coconut Chutney (30g): 62 kcal, 0.6g protein, 2.0g carbs, 5.8g fat (MCT fatty acids)
- Peanut Chutney (30g): 72 kcal, 3.5g protein, 3.0g carbs, 5.5g fat (vitamin E, niacin)

## Functional Indian Foods with Evidence-Based Health Benefits

### Anti-Inflammatory and Metabolic Spices
- Turmeric (Haldi): curcumin at 3-5% concentration; anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective; bioavailability enhanced 2000% when consumed with black pepper (piperine)
- Fenugreek (Methi): galactomannan fibre slows gastric emptying; 4-hydroxyisoleucine stimulates insulin secretion
- Cinnamon (Dalchini): cinnamaldehyde improves peripheral glucose uptake; 1-3g daily may reduce fasting glucose by 10-18 mg/dL in some studies
- Ginger (Adrak): gingerols reduce postprandial glycaemia; anti-emetic and anti-inflammatory properties
- Black Pepper (Kali Mirch): piperine enhances nutrient absorption (curcumin, beta-carotene, CoQ10)
- Cumin (Jeera): improves lipid profile; traditional digestive carminative

### Nuts and Seeds
- Almonds (100g): 579 kcal, 21.2g protein, 21.7g carbs, 49.9g fat; vitamin E, magnesium; 30g daily recommended
- Walnuts (100g): 654 kcal, 15.2g protein, 13.7g carbs, 65.2g fat; highest plant source of omega-3 (ALA)
- Flaxseeds / Alsi (100g): 534 kcal, 18.3g protein, 28.9g carbs, 42.2g fat; richest plant omega-3 source
- Pumpkin Seeds (100g): 559 kcal, 30.2g protein, 10.7g carbs, 49.1g fat; zinc and magnesium dense
- Chia Seeds (100g): 486 kcal, 16.5g protein, 42.1g carbs, 30.7g fat; 34.4g fibre

## Cereal-Pulse Complementation (Amino Acid Synergy)
- Cereals (rice, wheat, millets) are low in lysine but adequate in methionine
- Pulses (dal, rajma, chana) are low in methionine but adequate in lysine
- Combining cereal + pulse in a 4:1 ratio achieves near-complete amino acid profile
- Traditional Indian meals inherently follow this pattern: rice + sambar, roti + dal, idli + sambar, khichdi
- This complementation makes vegetarian Indian diets nutritionally competitive with omnivorous diets when planned correctly
- Recommendation: include at least 2-3 servings of pulses daily for protein adequacy (ICMR-NIN, 2020)
